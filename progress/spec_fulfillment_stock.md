# Spec pass — `fulfillment_stock` (feature 17, phase 9)

**Status:** `spec_ready` — 2026-08-21. Deliverables: `specs/fulfillment_stock/{requirements,design,tasks}.md`, `feature_list.json` flipped to `spec_ready`. No edit to `specs/shared/` (the R30–R36/R61 rows already exist and carry names; this feature only flips statuses at close-out).

**Shared R-range:** `R30` – `R36`, `R61` (`specs/shared/requirements.md` §4), elaborated by `domain-model.md` §4, `saga.md` §2/§4.1/§6 and the five `fulfillment.stock.*` channels of `asyncapi.yaml`. `R36` is feature 18's responder (only `consume()` lands here); `R61`'s API half is feature 25's. Local requirements: `FS1` – `FS17` (two of them, `FS1`/`FS2`, are Orders-side).

## 1. Shape of the spec, in one paragraph

A pure `StockItem` aggregate (F1 in the aggregate, as phase 6 decided; F2 preserved by co-locating every counter change with its reservation change) with a `Reservation` child entity and a pure domain service that makes reservation all-or-nothing across an order's lines and builds the three facts; five `@MessagePattern(…, Transport.NATS)` responders on the `@nestjs/cqrs` `QueryBus` (check, list) and `CommandBus` (reserve, release, replenish); one short transaction per command that locks the order's stock rows `FOR UPDATE` in `(company_code, product_code)` index order before anything else — which is what makes two concurrent reserves for the last units resolve to exactly one `stock.reserved.v1` and one `stock.rejected.v1`, and what makes the `orderReference`-scoped responder idempotency (`already_reserved`, `already_released`) race-free; facts leave through Fulfillment's own copies of the outbox recorder/relay/publisher to `otc.fulfillment.facts.v1`. Two Orders changes go first and stay bounded: the D1 idempotent `enqueue` and the `x-correlation-id`/`x-request-id` headers without which Fulfillment cannot stamp `correlationId = orderId` on its facts. The live first boot is designed (§11): the three parked orders advance to `stock_reserved` and park at `credit.hold`.

| 11 | Lock protocol: per-row locks in request order (deadlock-prone), a table lock, or `SKIP LOCKED`? | **One `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` with `ORDER BY (company_code, product_code)`** over the distinct product codes — InnoDB takes the locks in unique-index order, so two multi-line reserves always lock in the same global order; the order's existing reservations are read `FOR UPDATE` **after** the stock locks so the idempotency check is a current read. Release pre-reads the stock ids without locking, then locks stock first, then reservations — same global order. Contenders **wait** (no `SKIP LOCKED`: skipping would reject an order that could have been served a few ms later). Deadlock shape tested explicitly (`[P1,P2]` vs `[P2,P1]`, 10×). | requirements `FS6`; design §4.3, §4.4; tasks D2, G4 |
